Your team and your roles

Work in teams of three. Every round has a planner, a picker and a recorder, and the roles rotate so everyone does each job once.

Roles in this round

Rotation

Planner

Sets up the experiment

Keeps the supermarket model up to date, draws the round card, gets the order, makes the plan and writes down a prediction before the run. Talks to the AI when the card says so.

Picker

Executes the plan

Takes the cart and picks every line in the real supermarket in the planned sequence, says "picked" out loud for each line and returns to the start. Does not look at predictions.

Recorder

Collects the data

Runs the stopwatch in run mode, reads the next location aloud, taps Picked for every line and writes one sentence about what happened.

How a round works

  1. The planner draws the round card and gets the order in tab 3.
  2. The planner makes the plan the card asks for and writes a prediction.
  3. The recorder opens run mode, the picker picks, the recorder taps each line.
  4. You compare prediction and reality, then rotate roles for the next round.

Waiting for your turn in the supermarket? Use the time for the Model tab or the AI tab.

Build the digital supermarket

Make the app match the real supermarket in the lab. Count the sections and levels, measure one section, then tap a slot to name what is in that bin.

Location codes read rack, section, level. R2-3 is the right rack, section 2 counted from the start, level 3 counted from the floor.

We assume heavy items sit on level 1, the floor level. The AI goals and the model use this.

Tip for the planner. If you change the real supermarket during the lab, for example to move fast movers closer to the start, change it here too, or the model will be wrong.

Simulation model

The model walks a virtual picker through your supermarket. It learns walking speed and pick times from your recorded runs, then lets you test plans before anyone walks.

What the model knows

What a model needs
  • Layout. Where every item is, how far apart the sections are.
  • Process times. How fast people walk, how long a pick takes at each level.
  • Variation. How much those times change from pick to pick and person to person.
  • Rules. The plan and the number of pickers.

Every number above comes from your own runs, so the model gets better each round.

Set up your experiment

The model runs the same plan many times with the variation you measured, so you see a range and not one number.
