Technical details

Posture snapshot

Frame the full body, preferably from the side.

2D posture bands only. These are not full RULA/REBA scores or a workplace risk assessment.

What is measured?

Trunk inclination from image vertical; upper-arm elevation relative to the torso; knee flexion = 180° minus the joint angle. Hidden joints show —.

RULA-informed bands: upper arm 0–20°, 20–45°, 45–90°, >90°; trunk 0–20°, 20–60°, >60°. A side view is needed to interpret these as forward flexion. Front views, extension and camera tilt change their meaning.

A full RULA assessment also needs neck, wrist, twist, support, force/load and muscle use. No automatic full score is produced.

Try a no-touch experiment
  1. Fix the camera and rename three visible zones.
  2. Record a short receiving → workstation → dispatch route. Compare logged times with a stopwatch.
  3. Repeat an unloaded reach at two comfortable heights. Compare posture-band exposure.
  4. Try two people, cross paths or hide the feet. Inspect gaps and temporary ID changes.
  5. Which manual booking could a zone event support? What evidence is still missing?

Temporary IDs are not identity recognition. Crossing people and occlusion can swap or reset IDs. Paths break across gaps rather than inventing movement.

Images stay on this device. Use consenting participants and comfortable, unloaded movements. No video is saved.
Model, method & limits

MediaPipe Pose Landmarker Lite, Tasks Vision 0.10.21. Up to three poses with simple proximity IDs; no persistent identity. Angle bands refer to RULA posture categories (McAtamney & Corlett, 1993), but omit essential components and adjustments. Band exposure counts valid observed intervals only; it is not a validated safety metric. Camera motion, out-of-plane movement and occlusion affect every result. The example is synthetic and is labelled in exports.
