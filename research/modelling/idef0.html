IDEF0 suggestion – AMR material transport & charging

This page proposes a multi-level IDEF0 model derived from your activity flow (Pick → Load → Transport → Unload → Charge → Idle). It restructures the logic into valid IDEF0 functions with clear ICOM (Input / Control / Output / Mechanism) definitions and interfaces between functions. Use it as a template for drawing A-0, A0, and A1/A2 diagrams in an IDEF0 tool.

IDEF0 reminder: IDEF0 is not a sequence diagram. Outputs of one function may become inputs/controls of another, but the diagram itself should not imply time order. If you want sequencing, keep it for a DES/process-flow view in addition to IDEF0.

A-0 (Context): Manage AMR-based material transport and charging

One top-level function showing the system boundary and the main ICOM.

A-0

Manage AMR-based material transport & charging

Inputs I
  • Transport requests / orders (pickup–delivery pairs)
  • Materials to be moved (unit loads / pallets / bins)
  • Battery energy demand (implicit from mission needs)
Controls C
  • Operational policies (dispatching, priority rules)
  • Charging policy (thresholds, schedule, charger allocation)
  • Layout & travel rules (network, speed limits, safety constraints)
  • Resource constraints (station capacity, charger availability)
  • KPIs / performance targets (throughput, energy, utilisation)
Outputs O
  • Delivered materials at destination stations
  • AMR availability status (idle/busy/charging)
  • Execution logs / performance measures
Mechanisms M
  • AMR(s) and onboard controllers
  • Charging infrastructure
  • WMS/MES/dispatcher (order release & assignment)
  • Operators/maintenance (as needed)

A0 (Decomposition): Core functions

Suggested A0 breakdown that maps to your activities but keeps IDEF0 functional abstraction. (You can rename functions to match your course conventions.)

A1

Plan & assign missions

Controls C
  • Dispatching/priority rules

  • Mission completion status

A3

Manage energy (charge decision & charging)

Inputs I

Controls C
  • Charging policy (thresholds, schedules, priorities)
  • Charger capacity/availability
  • Energy safety constraints
Outputs O
  • Charging assignment / charging plan
  • Updated battery state (charged AMR)
  • AMR availability status (charging/unavailable)
Mechanisms M
  • Charger(s)
  • Battery management system

A4

Manage availability (idle, staging, exceptions)

Inputs I
  • AMR status updates (mission complete, charged)
  • No-mission condition (empty queue)
Controls C
  • Idle/staging policy (where to wait)
  • Parking capacity constraints
  • Exception handling rules (blocked path, station busy)
Outputs O
  • AMR positioned in idle/staging area
  • Availability signal to dispatcher
  • Exception tickets / alerts (optional)
Mechanisms M
  • AMR(s)
  • Fleet manager / monitoring dashboard

Suggested interfaces (how A0 functions connect)

A1 → A2: Assigned mission (Output of A1) becomes an Input to A2.
A2 → A1: Mission completion status and AMR availability (Outputs of A2) become Controls/Inputs for future assignment in A1.
A3 ↔ A1/A2: Charging assignment and AMR unavailable (charging) (Outputs of A3) constrain A1 (Control) and may preempt A2 (Control). Conversely, SoC readings and mission schedule from A1/A2 inform A3 (Inputs/Controls).
A4 ↔ A1: Availability signal (Output of A4) is an Input to A1; no-mission condition and queue state from A1 are Controls to A4.
A2 ↔ A4: Exception tickets (Output of A4) can act as Controls on A2 (e.g., blocked station), and mission complete (Output of A2) triggers A4’s idle/staging (Input).
Tip: if you want to keep “Go to charger” and “Go to idle place” explicitly, you can place them as subfunctions under A3 (energy) and A4 (availability), respectively.

Interfaces inside A2 (without implying sequence)

A2.1 → A2.2: AMR at pickup location is an Input to A2.2.
